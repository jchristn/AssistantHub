Modern Slavery and Human Trafficking Statement FY2024/25

Meridian Instruments Ltd. Financial year 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025. Published 15 September 2025. Owner: Legal and Compliance (CC-5200), with Procurement (CC-1210).

This statement is made under section 54 of the UK Modern Slavery Act 2015 and sets out the steps Meridian Instruments Ltd and its subsidiaries took during the financial year ending 31 March 2025 to prevent modern slavery and human trafficking in our operations and supply chains. It was approved by the Board of Directors on 9 September 2025.

1. Our business and supply chain

Meridian Instruments Ltd is a designer and manufacturer of industrial sensing equipment with approximately 1,400 employees. Our headquarters are in Leeds, United Kingdom, and we operate sites in Rotterdam (Netherlands), Austin (United States) and Penang (Malaysia). Our product lines are the Halcyon gas-detector family, the Tessera vibration-monitoring platform and the Lumen optical-flow meters.

Final assembly and test take place in Leeds and Penang. Our supply chain includes electronic component distributors, printed circuit board assemblers, sensor element manufacturers, precision machining and plastics suppliers, packaging, logistics, facilities services, cleaning, security and temporary labour agencies.

2. Policies

Our approach is set out in the following policies:

  • Code of Conduct (POL-CORP-001), which prohibits forced, bonded, trafficked and child labour in any form;
  • Procurement Manual (POL-FIN-012), which includes the Supplier Code of Conduct and ethical sourcing requirements;
  • Speak Up Policy (POL-CORP-004), which allows workers, suppliers and their employees to raise concerns confidentially;
  • Recruitment and Selection Manual (POL-HR-052), which governs our own hiring and use of agencies.

3. Risk assessment

During FY2024/25 Procurement screened 312 tier-1 suppliers against country, sector and commodity risk indicators. Of these, 41 were rated high risk. The main risk areas identified were:

  • electronic component and sub-assembly manufacturing in South-East and East Asia;
  • migrant labour recruited through agents for manufacturing work in Malaysia, including at our own Penang site;
  • outsourced cleaning, security and catering services at all four sites;
  • logistics and warehousing.

4. Due diligence and audits

High-risk suppliers were asked to complete a labour standards questionnaire, and we carried out 18 on-site audits during the year, conducted either by our Penang Quality Assurance team (CC-3110) or by an independent social auditor.

The most common findings were excessive overtime, retention of workers' passports by employers or agents, and incomplete wage records. One supplier of cable harnesses was exited after it failed to return retained passports within the agreed timeframe.

5. Our own operations: the Employer Pays Principle in Penang

Meridian applies the Employer Pays Principle: no worker should pay for a job. All recruitment fees and related costs for workers at our Penang site must be borne by Meridian or the labour agent, not by the worker.

In FY2024/25 an internal review of labour agent practices found that some migrant workers placed at Penang by one agent had paid recruitment fees in their home country before arrival. Through the labour agent remediation programme, recruitment fees totalling RM 94,300 were reimbursed to 23 migrant workers. The agent's contract was amended to require fee-free recruitment, with quarterly worker interviews carried out independently of the agent.

Workers at Penang keep their own passports. Secure personal lockers are provided on site, and passports are never held by Meridian or its agents.

6. Agency and contract labour at our other sites

At Leeds, Rotterdam and Austin, temporary workers are engaged only through agencies on the preferred supplier list managed by Procurement. Agencies must confirm that workers are paid directly, that no fees are charged to them, and that working hours comply with local law. During the year we reviewed the contracts of our outsourced cleaning and security providers at all four sites and added a right to interview their workers on our premises without supervisors present.

7. Training

All employees complete the Code of Conduct training, which includes a modern slavery module. In FY2024/25 an additional in-depth session was delivered to 64 employees in Procurement, Quality Assurance, Facilities and the Penang People team, focusing on indicators of forced labour and on how to handle a disclosure.

8. Grievance mechanisms

Concerns can be raised through the Meridian Speak Up channels described in the Speak Up Policy (POL-CORP-004), which are available to suppliers' workers as well as our own. Channels are available in English, Dutch and Malay. Posters in Penang production areas are also displayed in Bahasa Indonesia, Nepali and Burmese.

9. Measuring effectiveness and next steps

In FY2025/26 we will:

  • extend screening to selected tier-2 suppliers of printed circuit board assemblies;
  • complete all open corrective action plans;
  • require all labour agents supplying the Penang site to sign the updated fee-free recruitment clause;
  • report progress to the Audit Committee every six months.
