# Whistleblowing Policy

Policy code: POL-CORP-004. Owner: Company Secretary, Legal and Compliance (CC-5200). Effective date: 1 June 2022. Version: 1.

## 1. Purpose

Meridian Instruments Ltd is committed to conducting its business honestly and with integrity. This policy encourages employees and others working for Meridian to report genuine concerns about wrongdoing as early as possible, explains how to do so, and describes the protection available to those who raise concerns in good faith.

## 2. Who this policy applies to

This policy applies to all employees, officers, contractors, agency workers, interns and consultants at all Meridian sites: Leeds, Rotterdam, Austin and Penang.

## 3. What to report

Whistleblowing is the disclosure of information relating to suspected wrongdoing or danger at work. Examples include:

- criminal activity, including fraud, theft, bribery and corruption;
- failure to comply with a legal or regulatory obligation;
- danger to health and safety, including unsafe handling of calibration gases or test equipment;
- damage to the environment;
- falsification of product test or certification records;
- breaches of our Code of Conduct (POL-CORP-001) or financial controls;
- deliberate concealment of any of the above.

Personal grievances about your own employment terms, such as a dispute about pay or a disagreement with your manager, should normally be raised under the Disciplinary and Grievance Procedure (POL-HR-040) instead.

## 4. How to raise a concern

In most cases you should first raise your concern with your line manager. If you do not feel able to do so, or you believe your manager is involved, you can use any of the following channels:

- email the ethics mailbox at ethics@meridian-instruments.example, which is read only by the Company Secretary and the Head of Internal Audit;
- call the independent Clearline Ethics hotline on 0800 915 4420, available 24 hours a day, which accepts anonymous reports;
- write to the Company Secretary at Meridian Instruments Ltd, Leeds headquarters, marked "Private and confidential";
- if your concern relates to a member of the executive team, contact the Chair of the Audit Committee through the Company Secretary.

Colleagues at the Rotterdam, Austin and Penang sites may also call the Clearline Ethics hotline, which offers international call-back on request.

## 5. Confidentiality and anonymity

We hope you will feel able to raise concerns openly. If you ask us to keep your identity confidential, we will not disclose it without your consent unless required by law. Anonymous reports will be considered, but they can be harder to investigate because we cannot ask follow-up questions.

## 6. What happens next

1. The Company Secretary will acknowledge your concern within 7 days of receipt.
2. An initial assessment will determine whether an investigation is needed and who should carry it out.
3. You may be asked to attend a meeting to provide further information. You may bring a colleague or trade union representative.
4. We aim to give you feedback on the outcome within 3 months, although we may not be able to share full details for reasons of confidentiality.

## 7. Protection and support

No one who raises a genuine concern will suffer any detriment as a result, even if the concern turns out to be mistaken. Anyone found to have victimised or retaliated against a whistleblower will face disciplinary action. Deliberately making false allegations is also a disciplinary matter.

## 8. External disclosures

The aim of this policy is to provide an internal mechanism for reporting and investigating wrongdoing. In some circumstances the law recognises that it may be appropriate to report concerns to an external body, such as a regulator. We strongly encourage you to seek advice before reporting a concern externally.

## 9. Responsibility and review

The Company Secretary has overall responsibility for this policy and reports whistleblowing statistics to the Audit Committee twice a year. This policy will be reviewed every three years.
